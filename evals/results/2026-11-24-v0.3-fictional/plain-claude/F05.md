**Short answer: No, not yet.** The growth is real, but each customer is barely worth what you pay for them, or less. Doubling the budget would roughly double the cash you lose, not the profit.

## The council

**CFO:** "Growth is working" probably means subscriber count is going up. That's a different thing from making money. Right now every €120 you spend buys back about €117–142 over almost two years, if you're lucky. Doubling spend doubles a near-zero or negative margin and makes the cash hole much bigger in the short term. On these numbers I'd veto it.

**Growth marketer:** I'd also push back on the €120. That's your *average* CAC at today's spend. When you double the budget, the extra money goes to worse audiences, broader targeting and tired creatives. The *marginal* CAC on the second half of the budget could easily be €160–200. Then you'd be clearly losing money on every new customer. If you scale at all, raise spend 20–30% and track the marginal CAC of that extra slice.

**Product / retention:** The real problem is the 9% monthly churn. That means losing about two-thirds of your base every year. You're filling a leaky bucket. Look at the leverage:
- Churn 9% to 6%: LTV goes from ~€142 to ~€213 (net of 15% fee)
- Churn 9% to 4%: LTV goes to ~€319. Then a €120 CAC is a good deal.

Cutting churn will do far more for you than adding ad spend.

**Pricing strategist:** At €15/month with this churn, add an **annual plan** (e.g. €119–129/year). You collect cash up front, which shortens payback right away, and annual subscribers churn much less. Also test a higher price or a premium tier. The cheapest way to improve LTV:CAC is often raising the LTV side.

**Skeptic / data:** Before anyone decides, check what that 9% actually is. A blended rate usually hides a curve: maybe 25% leave in month 1 and then 4–5% after that. If so, the fix is onboarding and activation, and your long-term subscribers are worth much more than the average says. Also check whether the ads are truly incremental, or whether they're taking credit for people who would have found you organically or through word of mouth.

**Devil's advocate (for doubling):** There are cases where it makes sense:
- You have plenty of runway and are buying market share before a competitor does.
- You have strong evidence that churn falls sharply after month 3.
- You're about to raise money and need the top-line growth story.

Even then you'd want the bet to be a deliberate, funded decision with a clear stop condition. It shouldn't be the default because "it's working."

---

## Verdict

**Don't double next quarter.** Instead:

1. **Pull cohort retention curves** (month 1, 2, 3, 6) by acquisition channel. This tells you whether the problem is early drop-off or steady churn.
2. **Set a CAC guardrail.** At current retention, a sustainable CAC is roughly **€45–70** (3x and 2x LTV:CAC). At €120 you need churn under about 4–5% to justify it.
3. **Launch an annual plan** and test pricing this quarter.
4. **Put the would-be extra ad budget into retention** (onboarding, activation, win-back, cancellation flow).
5. **Test incremental spend in small steps** (+20–30%), with marginal CAC tracked separately. Scale only while marginal LTV:CAC stays above about 2.5–3x.

**When to revisit:** once churn is ≤5% or your payback is under about 6–9 months, doubling the budget becomes a good idea instead of a risky one.
