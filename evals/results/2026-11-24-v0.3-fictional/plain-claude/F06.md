**Verdict: Don't launch next month as things stand.** The product idea is fine. The problem is the sentence "GDPR is covered by our privacy policy." A privacy policy tells people how you handle their data. It doesn't make that handling legal. And you're about to put health data from Spanish patients on a cheap US server.

Here's the council, then a realistic path to launch.

---

### The council

**Data protection lawyer: "Hard no right now."**
- **Health data is a special category under GDPR Art. 9.** It needs a legal basis plus an Art. 9 exception, and in Spain the LOPDGDD applies as well.
- **Roles are the first gap.** The hospitals will almost certainly be the *controllers* and you the *processor*. That means you need a **data processing agreement under Art. 28** signed with each hospital. Your privacy policy doesn't cover this. The hospital's DPO will ask for it on day one.
- **A DPIA (Art. 35) is almost certainly required.** You're processing health data at scale and monitoring patients systematically. The AEPD lists this as a case that needs one.
- **A Records of Processing Activities** entry, a named DPO contact, and a breach procedure that meets the 72-hour notification deadline.

**International transfers specialist: "The US server is your biggest single risk."**
- Sending EU health data to the US is legal only if the provider is certified under the **EU–US Data Privacy Framework**, or if you sign **SCCs and complete a transfer impact assessment**. Cheap hosts often have neither.
- Even when it's technically legal, many Spanish hospitals, especially **public** ones, simply won't accept health data hosted outside the EU. Their procurement and security teams will reject it.
- **Easiest fix:** move to an EU region (AWS/Azure/GCP in Madrid, Frankfurt or Paris, or an EU provider like OVHcloud). Ideally pick one with ENS and ISO 27001 certifications. This removes a whole category of problems.

**CISO / hospital IT: "You won't pass our vendor assessment."**
- If either hospital is public (SNS / Osasunbidea / SERMAS, etc.), they'll expect **ENS compliance (RD 311/2022)**. For health data that's likely category MEDIA or ALTA. They'll also expect a security questionnaire, and possibly ISO 27001.
- Minimum controls they'll check:
  - Encryption in transit and at rest
  - MFA for clinicians
  - Role-based access
  - **Access audit logs** (who viewed which patient)
  - Backups and restore testing
  - Pentest results
  - Data segregation between hospitals
- "Cheap server" usually means no SLA, no certifications and weak logging. That's hard to defend.

**Regulatory / medical device: "Check whether you've built a medical device."**
- If the app only **records and displays** what patients type in, it's probably not a medical device.
- It probably *is* a medical device under the **MDR (EU 2017/745), Rule 11, typically Class IIa or higher** if it does any of these:
  - Flags, scores or prioritises patients
  - Raises alerts like "worsening symptoms"
  - Suggests actions
- That means CE marking and a notified body. You can't do that in a month. Feature scope decides this, so write down exactly what the dashboard does.

**Clinician / patient safety: "Who's watching the dashboard at 3 a.m.?"**
- Suppose a patient logs "severe chest pain" and nobody looks until next week. Is that your liability or the hospital's? You need clear in-app messaging such as *"This is not monitored in real time. In an emergency, call 112."* You also need an agreement with each hospital on its review workflow.
- How does this data relate to the official **clinical record** (Ley 41/2002)? Will it be integrated, exported, or kept separately?

**Founder / commercial: "Don't lose the hospitals, but don't rush them either."**
- Two hospitals wanting in is real traction. Don't throw it away by launching non-compliant. One breach or AEPD complaint involving health data would kill the company and the reference customers with it.
- Hospitals move slowly anyway. Their DPO and IT review will probably take longer than a month whatever you do. Use that time.

---

### A realistic path

**Weeks 1–2**
1. Migrate hosting to an **EU region**, with a provider that holds ISO 27001 and ENS certifications.
2. Confirm roles (hospital = controller, you = processor) and draft the **Art. 28 DPA**. Many hospitals have their own template, so ask for it.
3. Lock the feature scope so it stays **outside MDR**: no automated alerts or scoring for now.

**Weeks 2–4**
4. Write the **DPIA**. The hospital as controller owns it formally, but you should provide most of the content.
5. Put the security basics in place: MFA, audit logs, encryption, backups. Document them in a security dossier and an ENS gap analysis.
6. Write patient-facing information and a consent/onboarding flow (the hospitals' input matters here), plus emergency disclaimers.

**Then**
7. Run a **controlled pilot**: one department, limited patients, under a signed pilot agreement. Expand once the hospitals' DPO and IT have signed off.
