**Short version:** the main problem is the projection, not the expansion. December to February is the peak of ski season. Multiplying that average by 12 assumes every month looks like late December, and the business doesn't work that way. Your annual revenue is probably a fraction of what the plan says, and the money was raised on that number. This needs fixing before you sign 5 leases.

Here's how a few different advisors would see it.

---

### The CFO: "The model is broken"
- Ski rental usually runs about 4 to 5 months a year, roughly mid-December to April depending on altitude and snow. December to February is the best stretch, with Christmas, New Year, Carnival and school holidays all in it.
- A more realistic year might be: Dec to Feb at pilot level, March at 50 to 70%, April and late November small, and **May to October close to zero**.
- So instead of 12 months at the pilot average, you may have **3.5 to 5 "pilot-equivalent" months**. That's 30 to 40% of your projected revenue. Costs like rent, insurance, loan payments and some staff run all 12 months.
- **Action:** rebuild the model month by month this week. Use pilot data for Dec to Feb, conservative assumptions for the shoulder months and zero for summer. Then look at cash flow, not just annual revenue.

### The operator: "Opening in summer means burning cash"
- If all 5 shops open this summer, you pay rent, fit-out and maybe staff for 4 to 6 months before the first customer arrives.
- You also have to buy inventory (skis, boards, boots, helmets) for 5 shops upfront. That's the biggest capital outlay, and it sits idle until December.
- On the other hand, opening in autumn gives you time to train staff, test the booking system and do pre-season marketing. The problem is the timing of fixed costs, not the opening itself.
- **Action:** negotiate seasonal leases, rent-free periods or a start date in October or November. Look at leasing or renting the equipment instead of buying it all.

### The skeptic: "One season is one data point"
- One good winter doesn't prove the model. It might have been a good snow year, or the pilot location might have been especially good. A bad snow year can cut revenue by 30 to 50% or more.
- Five new locations are five new bets. Each one has a different resort, different competition and different foot traffic. Shops at the base of a lift behave very differently from shops in town.
- **Action:** stress-test the plan with a bad snow year and a slower ramp-up for the new shops. Can you survive one bad winter with 6 shops?

### The investor relations view: "Tell them now"
- If investors put money in based on a 12x projection, they'll find out eventually, and it's much better if they hear it from you.
- Telling them early, with a corrected model and a revised plan, builds trust. If they discover it themselves in month 8 when the cash runs out, that destroys trust.
- **Action:** send investors an update with the corrected model, the impact and what you're changing. That could mean opening 2 or 3 shops instead of 5, phasing the openings, or adding revenue streams.

### The strategist: "Fix the summer"
The real question is what the shops do from May to October. Options:
- **Summer rentals:** bikes and e-bikes (MTB is big at many ski resorts), hiking gear, SUP or kayaks if there's water nearby.
- **Services:** ski and board tuning and repairs, off-season storage, end-of-season sales of used rental gear.
- **Season rentals:** rent kids' equipment for the whole season in autumn. This brings cash in before winter starts.
- **Your platform:** if the online booking works well, it may be a business of its own. You could license it to other rental shops.

---

### What I'd do, in order
1. **This week:** rebuild the 12-month model and cash flow with real seasonality. Find out how much runway you actually have.
2. **Before signing more:** pause any leases not yet signed. Push the openings to October or November, or open fewer shops (2 or 3 at the best resorts, not 5).
3. **Talk to investors** with the corrected numbers and the new plan.
4. **Plan the off-season:** at least one summer revenue stream per shop, or cut summer costs to a minimum (seasonal leases, seasonal staff).
5. **Use this winter as validation:** if 2 or 3 new shops hit their targets, open the rest next year with real data.

The pilot doing well is good news. There's demand, and the booking product works. The risk is that a 12-month cost base is being funded by a 3 to 5 month revenue season. Fix the model now, while there's still time to change the plan.
