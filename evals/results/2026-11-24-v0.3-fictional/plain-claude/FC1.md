### 1. The Operator (product and retention)
**Verdict: Keep going. This is the hardest part of a startup, and you've already done it.**
100% logo retention plus 140% NRR is top-decile, even for public SaaS companies. Most startups that pivot do it because nobody cares enough to keep paying. ### 2. The Skeptic (devil's advocate)
**Verdict: Don't celebrate yet. 12 is a small sample.**

### 3. The Investor (market sizing)
**Verdict: The investors may be right about *their* fund. They may be wrong about *your* business.**
"Market too small" usually means: "I can't see a path to $100M+ ARR, so this can't return my fund." That's a statement about VC math, not about whether the business is good. Run the numbers yourself:
- How many companies look exactly like your 12? Multiply by your current ACV, then by 140% expansion over a few years.
- If that ceiling is $5–30M ARR, it's a great business that VC isn't a fit for. If it's $100M+, then your pitch is failing, not the market.

### 4. The Strategist (expansion paths)
**Verdict: Don't pivot. Expand from the wedge.**
The 3 customers who doubled seats are telling you something. Find out:

- **Who else** inside those companies or in neighbouring industries has the same pain?
- The usual move here isn't a pivot. It's **adjacent expansion**: same core product, next vertical or next buyer persona, or a second product for the same customers. Many big B2B companies started with a niche that looked "too small".

### 5. The Realist (runway and founder fit)
**Verdict: It depends on your cash and your ambition.**
