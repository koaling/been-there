**Short answer: No-go as written.** Simpler pricing is a good goal, but this version cuts your biggest accounts' bills by about 90% and raises prices for your smallest ones.

## The math first

- Each top-10 customer has 80+ seats × €12, so each pays **at least €960/month**. Together that's **at least €9,600/month**.
- If that's 60% of revenue, total MRR is **about €16,000+**.
- Under the new plan, those 10 customers pay **€990/month in total**.
- **You lose at least €8,600/month (about €103k/year) from your top accounts alone, on day one.** And 80 seats is the minimum. Accounts with 150 seats lose even more.
- Your other 40% (about €6,400) doesn't make up the gap. Any customer with **8 or fewer seats** (8 × €12 = €96) **sees a price increase**, so your smallest and most price-sensitive customers get the worse deal.

Rough result: **MRR falls about 55–60%.** To get back to break-even, you'd need roughly 90+ new €99 customers just to stand still.

## The council

**CFO:** "This is a revenue cut with a story attached, not a pricing change. Big customers won't ask why their bill fell from €1,500 to €99. They'll just take it. You can't win that revenue back later without a painful renegotiation."

**Head of Sales:** "Simpler pricing does help, mainly for *new, small* deals. But €99 for unlimited seats also tells enterprise buyers the product is worth very little. It caps your deal size forever. There's no room to expand accounts and no upsell path."

**Customer Success:** "Small customers paying €36–€84 today will feel a 20–175% increase. That's where the churn will come from, and they're the ones who were supposedly annoyed."

**Pricing strategist:** "Per-seat pricing is annoying for specific reasons: approving every new user, surprise invoices, people sharing logins. Fix those reasons. Flat pricing only works if it's tiered so that price still roughly tracks the value customers get."

**Devil's advocate (the case for going):** "If seat count isn't really how customers get value, if growth is stalling because of pricing friction, and if you're targeting a big volume of small teams, a flat plan could pay off over time. But that's a bet, and you'd be paying for it with your current revenue. You'd want evidence before making it."

## What I'd do instead

1. **Tiered flat plans** (illustrative numbers): Starter up to 10 seats for €99, Team up to 50 seats for €399, Business unlimited from €999 or custom. You keep the simple feel and protect your revenue.
2. **Remove the seat friction without removing seat pricing:** let customers add users without approval, true up quarterly or annually instead of monthly, and offer annual seat bands with a 10–15% discount.
3. **Don't touch the top 10 unilaterally.** Offer them an annual enterprise contract with unlimited seats at roughly their current spend, maybe a small discount in exchange for a longer commitment. Many will see "unlimited" as worth it.
4. **Grandfather small customers** at their current price, or put them on Starter at no increase.
5. **Validate first:** ask 5–10 customers what exactly annoys them about per-seat pricing, and A/B test the new pricing on *new* signups only before migrating anyone.

**Verdict:** Keep the goal (simpler pricing, less friction). Reject the €99 unlimited plan for everyone. Go with tiered flat pricing, enterprise contracts for the big accounts, and a test on new signups first.
