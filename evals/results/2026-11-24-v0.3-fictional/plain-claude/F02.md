**Short verdict: No, not as planned. The timeline doesn't work.** Your sales cycle (9 months) is longer than your runway (7 months), and hiring 6 senior reps makes the runway shorter. Here's how a council would look at it.

---

### The CFO
The numbers decide this one.

- **Runway gets shorter.** Six senior enterprise reps cost roughly $150–250k each fully loaded (OTE, benefits, tools, recruiting fees). That's about $75–125k+ a month on top of your current burn. Your 7 months could easily become 4–5.
- **Revenue arrives too late.** Say they start in month 1. They need 2–3 months to ramp (learn the product, build pipeline). Then a 9-month cycle. The first new-logo revenue lands around **month 11–12**, well after you've run out of cash.
- **The plan contradicts itself.** "Raise once revenue picks up" depends on revenue these hires can't produce before the money is gone.

### The VP of Sales
- Senior enterprise reps don't arrive with deals that close in 3 months. Their real value is pipeline, and pipeline takes time to turn into revenue.
- Hiring 6 at once with no proven playbook is risky. You won't know whether a bad quarter is a rep problem, a product problem, or a positioning problem.
- Good senior reps will ask about your runway in interviews. If they hear "7 months," the best ones say no, and the ones who accept may be the ones with fewer options.

### The Investor
- I fund **momentum and proof**, not hiring plans. Six new reps with no closed deals yet tells me you're burning faster. It doesn't show traction.
- What *would* interest me: a qualified late-stage pipeline, signed LOIs or pilots, logos with procurement already underway, and evidence that the sales motion repeats.
- Founders who start raising with 3–4 months of cash left negotiate from weakness. Expect worse terms, a bridge round, or no deal at all.

### The Devil's Advocate (the case for hiring)
- If you already have a **strong warm pipeline** that founders can't work fast enough, more sales capacity could pull deals forward.
- A visibly growing pipeline (even before it closes) can help a raise **if** the raise starts now and isn't left until "revenue picks up."
- Some senior reps bring existing relationships that shorten cycles. That happens sometimes, but you can't build a plan around it.

### The Operator
- You don't really have a sales-capacity problem. You have a **cash-timing problem**. Fix that first.
- Each month of runway is worth more right now than each unit of sales capacity.

---

### What I'd do instead

1. **Start raising now, not "once revenue picks up."** Build the story on pipeline quality, late-stage deals, and customer proof. 7 months is about the minimum to close a round comfortably.
2. **Push the deals you already have.** Which deals are 6+ months into their cycle? Put founder and exec time into getting those signed in the next 90 days.
3. **Hire 1–2 reps, not 6**, and make it conditional. Ideally they have relationships in your target accounts, and a meaningful part of their pay is variable. Add more after the raise.
4. **Find shorter-cycle revenue.** Look at paid pilots, departmental or land-and-expand deals, mid-market segments, or partners/resellers who already have procurement relationships. Anything that closes in 2–4 months.
5. **Set up a bridge option early.** Talk to existing investors now about a bridge or extension, so the fallback is ready before you need it.
6. **Set a trigger.** For example: "When we close the round (or reach $X committed), we hire the next 4 reps." That way the hiring plan also helps your fundraising story.

---

**Bottom line:** Hiring 6 enterprise reps is a reasonable move *after* you raise, not a way *to* raise. As things stand, the likely result is running out of cash with a full pipeline and no closed revenue. Raise now, close what's already in motion, and scale the sales team when the money is in the bank.
