Short answer: no, not as stated. Sellers would like it, but the numbers don't work. Here's how a small council of advisors would see it.

**The math first**

- Today: 15% × €40 = **€6.00 per order**
- Proposed: **€1.00 per order**
- That's an **83% cut in revenue per order**. To earn the same total you'd need **6× the order volume**, not 6× the sellers. More sellers only helps if buyer demand grows to match.

---

**The CFO**
"You're giving up €5 per order and hoping volume makes up for it. Payment processing alone (about 1.5–3% + a fixed fee, so roughly €0.80–€1.20 on a €40 order) could eat most or all of that €1. If you pay those fees, you may lose money on every transaction."

**The marketplace economist**
"Supply is rarely the constraint people think it is. Most marketplaces are limited by demand. Adding sellers without adding buyers spreads the same orders across more sellers. Each one sells less, and they leave anyway. Also, a buyer fee raises the price buyers see at checkout, which is the side you actually need to grow."

**The seller's advocate**
"Sellers will love it at first. But they don't choose a marketplace for the low fee. They choose it for sales. If the extra seller count doesn't bring more orders, 0% commission on fewer sales isn't a win for them. And some sellers will just add the old 15% back into their prices, so buyers pay more and you earn less."

**The buyer's advocate**
"A visible €1 fee at checkout hurts conversion more than a hidden commission does. It's a small amount, but fees shown at the end cause cart abandonment, especially on small orders. On a €10 order it's 10%."

**The devil's advocate (for the idea)**
"It isn't crazy as a **temporary or targeted** move. Zero commission for new sellers for 90 days, or in a category where you need supply, is a proven cold-start tactic. If supply really is your bottleneck (buyers are searching and not finding things), lowering the barrier for sellers can unlock growth. But that's a subsidy with an end date, not a business model."

---

**Verdict**

The plan swaps a model that makes money for one that probably barely covers payment costs. It also rests on two assumptions you haven't tested: that supply is your bottleneck, and that more sellers will bring 6× the orders.

**What I'd do instead**

1. **Check where the bottleneck is.** Look at searches with no results, unmet buyer demand, and sales per seller. If buyers can't find what they want, supply is the problem. If sellers already sit idle, it isn't.
2. **Lower, don't remove:** e.g. 8–10% commission, or tiered (lower for high-volume sellers).
3. **Time-boxed incentive:** 0% commission for the first 3 months or the first €X in sales for new sellers.
4. **Hybrid:** a small commission (5–8%) plus a small buyer fee, so neither side carries the full cost and your revenue stays healthy.
5. **Test it** on one category or region before rolling it out to everyone. Compare revenue per order, orders per seller, and buyer conversion.
