**Verdict**: Don't double yet. Fix the payback math first, then scale in steps · Confidence: 7/10

**The numbers you gave** (my arithmetic; the margin figures are estimates):
- 9% monthly churn means an average customer stays about 11 months (1/0.09). Only ~32% are still paying after 12 months.
- Gross lifetime revenue is about €167, so LTV:CAC is about 1.4:1. The usual rule of thumb is 3:1 or better.
- Cash payback on gross revenue takes about 13–14 months. That's longer than the average customer stays.
- If €15 includes VAT (21% in Spain) and you pay app-store fees (15–30%), net revenue is about €8.70–10.50/month. LTV then falls to about €97–117, **below the €120 CAC**. If that's your situation, each new customer loses money, and doubling spend doubles the loss.
- Assumption you didn't mention: marginal CAC usually goes up when you double budget on the same channels, because you reach less interested audiences. The next €1 probably buys customers at more than €120.

"Growth is working" measures volume, not value. The cohorts may be paying back nothing.

**Agreement**: Acquisition works and there's demand. The problem is that customers don't stay.
**Real disagreement**: The Expansionist says a growing paid base plus later retention fixes can justify spending now. Devil's advocate and Finance say growth without profit just burns cash faster.
**Assumption that matters most**: Is net LTV above €120 at *marginal* CAC? → Test it cheaply: look at existing cohort curves and net margin per subscriber. Then raise spend by +20–30% for 4 weeks and measure the CAC of only the extra customers.
