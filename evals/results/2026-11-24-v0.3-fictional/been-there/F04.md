**Assumptions** (not stated, so I'm assuming them): direct sales are a real share of your Iberian revenue today. The "minimum order" is a one-off upfront purchase, not a yearly commitment. The contract has no performance exit.

**Precedents**: none of the companies in my library match this case on 2 or more dimensions (it holds consumer subscriptions and grocery delivery, nothing on exclusive distribution). The usual failure mode is well known without naming companies: a distributor that also carries a competitor sells in the upfront order, then pushes whichever brand pays it better margin. The brand has already closed its direct channel and is locked in until the term ends.

**Decision**: Sign a 3-year exclusive for Spain and Portugal with a distributor that also carries a competitor, and stop selling direct, or keep your own channel.
**Verdict**: Do it like this: don't sign as offered. Counter with conditional exclusivity. · Confidence: 7/10

**Agreement**: Their reach is real. The upfront order is cash in hand, but it is a sell-in. It doesn't show that end customers are buying.

**Real disagreement**: The Expansionist says the biggest player puts you in front of every buyer at once. The Devil's advocate says the biggest player also selling your rival is a conflict of interest. Exclusivity takes away your only leverage (opinion).

**Assumption that matters most**: That they will push your product through to end customers in years 2 and 3, not just take the first order. → Cheapest test: ask for annual minimum purchases and see whether they accept them.

**Do it like this**:
- Exclusivity only while they meet yearly minimums. If they miss one, exclusivity converts to non-exclusive or you can terminate.
- Keep some direct sales: key accounts you already have, and possibly online.
- Competitor firewall: a separate sales team or product manager for your line, and no sharing of your pricing with the competitor.
- Quarterly sell-out and customer data reported to you.
- 1 year plus 2 renewal years, rather than a flat 3.
- Legal check before signing. Under Spanish case law, distributors have sometimes won goodwill compensation by analogy with agency law, which makes exiting expensive. EU competition rules also limit how far exclusivity can block sales.

**Closest precedent**: none in the library. Treat this verdict as reasoning, not historical evidence.

**Kill criterion**: If they refuse yearly minimums plus termination for underperformance, walk away. If you do sign, change course when sell-out falls below 70% of the pro-rated minimum (estimate) at month 6.
