**Decision**: Whether to swap a 15% seller commission (≈€6 per €40 order) for a flat €1 buyer fee to attract more sellers.
**Verdict**: Don't do it as stated. Do it like this: a time-boxed zero-commission offer for *new* sellers, plus a buyer fee of a fixed amount plus a percentage, but only after you confirm supply is really what holds you back. · Confidence: 7/10

**The number you didn't write down**: revenue per order falls from ~€6 to €1, an 83% cut. Just to stand still, order volume has to grow **6x**. Card processing will also come out of that €1 if you collect the payments (estimate: a few tens of cents on a €40 order in the EU), so net take could fall to around €0.50. A flat fee also doesn't scale with basket size. On a €200 order you'd earn €1 where you earn €30 today.

**Precedents** (the library has no direct match; one item comes from general knowledge and is marked)
☠ MoviePass (2017–19): cut price below unit cost to buy growth, and losses grew with every user. Matches: mechanism, growth-by-subsidy. Differs: market, model.
✓ Vinted (general knowledge, please verify current rates): zero seller commission, with the buyer paying a protection fee of a fixed amount **plus ~5%** of the item price. It made the model work because the fee scales with the order. Your flat €1 doesn't.

**Agreement**: seller-side fees really do hold back supply, and moving the fee to buyers is a proven model.
**Real disagreement**: whether supply is your constraint at all. If buyers aren't finding what they search for, more sellers help. If listings already go unsold, more sellers dilute sales per seller, and they leave anyway.
**Assumption that matters most**: "more sellers → 6x orders". Cheapest test: pull search-to-no-result rates and the share of listings with zero sales in 90 days. Ask 20 sellers who churned or never joined why. Was it the fee or the lack of buyers?
**Closest precedent**: MoviePass. You're not them yet, because you don't lose money per order. But you'd be paying for growth that may not come.
**One-way-door warning**: dropping commissions is easy. Bringing them back is not. Etsy's fee increases set off a seller strike in 2022.

**Next step (48 h)**: check zero-result searches and the share of listings with no sales in 90 days, then model revenue at 2x, 4x and 6x orders under the new fee.
**Kill criterion**: run zero commission for new sellers for 90 days. If orders per new seller don't reach at least the level of existing sellers, and total orders aren't on track for 6x, return to a lower commission (8–10%) rather than zero.

▸ Council
- Devil's advocate: against. An 83% revenue cut that depends on 6x growth nobody has shown.
- First principles: depends. Find out if supply is the bottleneck. Third option: cut the commission rather than kill it.
- Expansionist: for the direction. Vinted proves buyer-paid fees work, and a percentage-based fee keeps the upside.
- Outsider: a fee at checkout feels like a surprise. Show it up front.
- Executor: you can trial it for new sellers only, with no platform rebuild.
- Finance (specialist, because of the margin impact): a flat fee breaks on larger orders. Use fixed + %.
