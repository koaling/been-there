**Decision**: Whether to open all 5 new shops this summer on a plan that annualised a December–February pilot by multiplying the monthly average by 12.
**Verdict**: Don't open all 5 on this plan. Re-forecast first, then stage the openings. · Confidence: 8/10

**Agreement**: Every advisor flagged the same thing. December to February is the core of ski season and includes the Christmas holidays, so it is very likely your best stretch of the year. Multiplying it by 12 treats peak months as normal ones. In most snow destinations, ski rental makes most of its money in roughly 4–5 months and close to nothing for the rest of the year. Rent, insurance and equipment financing still have to be paid for all 12. So your projected annual revenue is probably several times too high (estimate; your own month-by-month data will give the real figure). Opening in summer also means each shop pays costs for about 5 months before it takes any meaningful revenue.

**Real disagreement**: The Expansionist says the pilot did prove real demand, and online booking can carry over to new locations. The Devil's advocate answers that one good season at one site doesn't tell you about a bad-snow year or how much any one site can earn. Both are right, and that is why the chair says stage the rollout, not cancel it.

**Assumption that matters most**: That one shop's full-year revenue covers its full-year fixed costs. → Cheapest test: rebuild the model month by month. Use pilot actuals for Dec–Feb, conservative figures for Mar–Apr, and zero for May–Nov unless you add a summer line (bike or e-bike rental is common in mountain towns). Then calculate break-even for each shop.

**Closest precedent**: ☠ Webvan. It expanded to new cities while its first market was still unprofitable and built capacity for demand that never came. Matches: the failure mechanism (premature scaling) and the model (owning physical locations before the economics were proven). Differs: market and scale. ✓ Instacart faced the same trap but entered new cities without building infrastructure first. Your version of that: take online bookings for the target towns this season through partner shops, hotels or pop-ups before you sign leases. You are not Webvan *yet*. The difference is whether you sign 5 leases before you know your real annual numbers.

**Investors**: They funded a plan based on the ×12 figure. Send them a corrected forecast now, before they find the error themselves. That protects trust, which you will need for later rounds.

**Next step (48 h)**: Produce the month-by-month model and break-even for each shop, and list which leases are already signed or still negotiable.

**Kill criterion**: If a shop's realistic full-year revenue doesn't cover its full-year costs with at least a 20% margin, don't open it. Settle this before signing each lease, and no later than 31 Jan 2027. Use mid-season data from this winter: your pilot shop, plus pre-bookings in the target towns.
