**Decision**: Decide whether 5 developers can, in one quarter, (a) rewrite the monolith into microservices, (b) launch the requested billing system and (c) keep shipping features.
**Verdict**: Don't do all three. Do it like this: ship billing and features on the monolith, and replace the full rewrite with a measured, incremental extraction only where data shows a bottleneck · Confidence: 8/10

*Assumptions (not stated): no dedicated ops/platform team, no hard evidence yet that the monolith is the scaling limit, billing touches core data (accounts, plans, invoices).*

**Agreement**: Billing is the only item with stated customer pull. It should get priority. A rewrite, a payments launch and a feature roadmap all compete for the same 5 people. Billing errors cost money and trust immediately.

**Real disagreement**: The Expansionist and Technical seats say some decoupling is worth doing now, because a clean billing boundary is a natural first service. The Devil's advocate says any architecture change this quarter puts billing at risk. Chair: build billing as a well-bounded module *inside* the monolith. You can extract it later at low cost, so you get the boundary without the risk.

**Assumption that matters most**: "Microservices = scale". The plan quietly assumes the monolith is what limits scale. Often it's a few slow queries, a missing cache or one hot path. Cheapest test: profile production for one week and name the top 3 bottlenecks. If none of them needs a service boundary, the rewrite has no business case.

**Closest precedent**: No strong match in the library. Webvan and Juicero share only the mechanism (infrastructure or complexity ahead of need), not the market. Well-known industry cases:
☠ Netscape: its from-scratch rewrite froze shipping for years while competitors moved, in Joel Spolsky's well-known analysis. Matches: mechanism and context (rewrite plus a frozen roadmap). Differs: market and scale.
☠ Segment: it publicly moved from microservices back to a monolith ("Goodbye Microservices", 2018) because operational overhead swamped a small team. Matches: team size relative to the number of services, and mechanism.
✓ Shopify: it scaled on a *modular monolith* instead of splitting into services.
You are not Shopify yet, because you have no module boundaries. You can be by the end of the quarter.

**Next step (48 h)**: Write a one-page capacity plan: 5 devs × ~12 weeks ≈ 60 dev-weeks, *minus* support and on-call (estimate). Allocate billing first, then features, then whatever is left for bottlenecks found by profiling. Start the 1-week production profiling in parallel.

**Kill criterion**: By week 4, if billing is behind its milestone, or if profiling shows the monolith is *not* the main constraint, drop all microservices work this quarter. If profiling shows one clear bottleneck, extract only that piece (strangler pattern), and only after billing is live.
