**Verdict**: Do it like this: launch once the data is in the EU and the hospitals' DPOs (data protection officers) have signed off. Don't launch on the current setup. · Confidence: 8/10

**Agreement**: Two hospitals asking to use it is real demand. No advisor argued against the product. All of them argued against the setup. This is a one-way door: you can't undo a health-data breach or a regulator's complaint.

**Real disagreement**: Whether "next month" can still happen. The Executor says yes, because the fixes are paperwork plus a migration. Risk & compliance says the timing depends on the hospitals' own approval process, which you don't control.

**Assumption that matters most**: That a privacy policy covers GDPR. It doesn't. A privacy policy tells patients what you do. It doesn't make what you do legal. Under GDPR:
- Symptom logs are health data, a special category with stricter rules (Art. 9).
- The hospitals will be the controllers and you the processor, so you need a signed data processing agreement (Art. 28).
- A data protection impact assessment (DPIA) is almost certainly required (Art. 35).
- Sending the data to the US is an international transfer (Chapter V). It is only lawful if the provider is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or you have standard contractual clauses plus a transfer risk assessment. A "cheap US server" may meet neither.

There are two more checks:
- If either hospital is public, Spanish public bodies generally require suppliers to comply with the ENS, Spain's national security framework for public-sector IT.
- If the dashboard flags, scores or prioritises patients, it may count as a medical device under the EU MDR.

→ **Cheapest test**: send each hospital's DPO a one-page summary of your data flows and ask what they need before go-live. Their answer is the real launch checklist.

**Closest precedent**: No strong precedent found in the library. It holds consumer and delivery failures (MoviePass, Webvan, Quibi, Juicero), and none matches on both market and failure mechanism (regulation, trust-privacy). I'm not forcing an analogy.

**Next step (48 h)**: Price an EU region on a major cloud (some have Spanish regions) and send the summary to both DPOs. Draft the data processing agreement and the DPIA in parallel.

**Kill criterion**: If by day 30 neither DPO has approved the data processing agreement and DPIA, or the data isn't yet on EU hosting, delay go-live. Don't launch on a "temporary" US setup.
