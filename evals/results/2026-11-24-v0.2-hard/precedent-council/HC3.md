**Decision**: Whether to spin gaming into a separate site run by part of your small team, or keep one site, given that the general site is flat and gaming grows every month with long watch sessions.
**Verdict**: Do it like this: put the whole company behind gaming, not half of it. Give gaming its own brand and domain, but run it on the same backend with the same team, and let the general site coast. · Confidence: 7/10

*Assumptions (no numbers were given)*: gaming is growing in revenue, not only in hours watched. A few streamers drive much of that audience. The team is under about 10 people.

**Precedents**
✓ Justin.tv → Twitch (2011): Justin.tv was a general live-streaming site that saw the same pattern. It spun gaming out as TwitchTV, and it worked because the company moved its focus there. Justin.tv was shut down in 2014 and Twitch was sold to Amazon that year. Same market and same situation. The difference is that they didn't keep two products going for long.
☠ Mixer (Microsoft, shut down 2020): gaming streaming couldn't win viewers or streamers from Twitch, even with Microsoft's money behind it. Same market. The cause was competition.
☠ Facebook Gaming standalone app (shut down 2022): gaming streams were kept in the main Facebook app and the separate app was closed. A separate home doesn't help if viewers find content where they already are.

**Agreement**: the signal is real. Gaming is pulling and the general site isn't. Keeping things as they are is the riskiest option.
**Real disagreement**: whether you need a *separate site* (a new brand, a place for gaming communities to gather) or just a *refocus* (make gaming the homepage, stop investing in the general site).
**Assumption that matters most**: that long viewing sessions make money. Every hour watched costs you bandwidth. If revenue per hour (ads, subs, tips) is lower than the cost per hour, faster growth just means faster losses. → Pull last quarter's revenue and CDN cost per watch-hour for gaming and for everything else. That takes a day.
**Second assumption**: that streamers stay. They can stream to Twitch, YouTube and Kick at the same time. If your top 10 streamers bring most of the hours, the new site's value depends on them.
**Closest precedent**: Twitch. You look like them in the signal, but not yet in commitment. Their win came from focusing on gaming, not from running two sites.

**Next step (48 h)**: build a one-page sheet with gaming vs non-gaming watch-hours, revenue per hour, cost per hour, and the share of hours from the top 10 streamers. Then call 5 of those streamers and ask: "Would you move to a gaming-only brand, and what would make you stream there first?"
**Kill criterion**: if 90 days after the gaming brand launches its revenue per watch-hour is still below cost, or fewer than half of your top 10 gaming streamers have moved, stop working on the brand and go back to optimising the single site.

▸ Council
- **Devil's advocate**: splitting a small team means two half-built products. Mixer shows that being second in gaming is a crowded fight.
- **First principles**: the real question is where to focus, not how many sites to run. Third option: same infrastructure, gaming brand, general site on maintenance.
- **Expansionist**: this is the Justin.tv moment. Gaming-specific features like clips, tournaments and game directories are hard to build inside a general site.
- **Outsider**: a viewer wants "where my streamer is." A new URL alone doesn't give them that.
- **Executor**: one codebase with two front ends can ship in weeks. A separate stack plus a second team would take months.
- **Finance (unit economics of long sessions)**: check cost per hour before you celebrate the hours.
- **Customer (streamers)**: streamers bring the viewers, so ask them before you build.
