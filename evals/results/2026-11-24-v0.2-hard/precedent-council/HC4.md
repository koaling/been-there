**Decision**: Decide whether to push streaming hard and accept that it will eat into the profitable DVD-by-mail business, or keep protecting DVD and let streaming grow at its own pace.

*Assumptions (no numbers given)*: DVD margins are healthy but the subscriber base is flat or ageing. Streaming is included in the subscription today. You don't have streaming rights to most of your catalogue yet. Note: if "now" means 2026, read the timing caveat at the end.

**Verdict**: Do it like this: cannibalise yourself on purpose, use DVD cash to pay for it, and move customers over gradually rather than forcing them with a price shock. · Confidence: 7/10

**Agreement**: The DVD business is a melting ice cube: discs, postage and warehouses are a cost floor streaming doesn't have. Waiting protects this year's profit and loses the decade.

**Real disagreement**: Pace. The Expansionist wants to migrate everyone fast. Finance and the Customer seat want DVD kept running as a cash engine, with no forced split.

**Assumption that matters most**: Streaming rights cost far more than discs. With a disc, you buy it once and can rent it out repeatedly. Streaming needs a licence for every title, and the price goes up when the licence is renewed. → Cheapest test: price streaming rights for your top 200 titles by usage before you commit spend.

**Closest precedent**: Netflix (2007–2011). It bundled streaming into DVD plans and won. In 2011 it then split the prices and announced Qwikster, which it reversed within weeks, and it lost about 800,000 US subscribers in one quarter. You are Netflix if you cannibalise gradually. You become Blockbuster if you protect the profitable core. Blockbuster was bankrupt in 2010.

Other precedents (from well-established history, not the precedent library):
☠ Blockbuster (2010): protected a profitable rental model. Matches: market, timing, incumbent mechanism. Differs: stores, not mail.
☠ Kodak (2012): invented the digital camera, then protected film. Matches: timing, mechanism. Differs: market.
Quibi (2020): a caution, not a death sentence. Streaming on its own creates no habit; content does.

**Next step (48 h)**: Split current subscribers into two groups: those who stream and those who don't. Compare churn and lifetime value. If the streaming group stays longer, that justifies the push.

**Kill criterion**: Change course if, after 6 months of the push, the streaming group's churn is no better than DVD-only's, or content cost per streaming subscriber is above DVD fulfilment cost with no downward trend.

▸ Council
- **Devil's advocate**: You'll pay rising licence fees for content you don't own. Price shocks kill trust (Netflix 2011).
- **First principles**: The product is "easy access to films", not discs. Keep DVD only for the long tail that streaming can't license.
- **Expansionist**: Every streaming subscriber cuts postage and shipping time. Whoever builds the habit first takes the market.
- **Outsider**: Customers just want to watch tonight. Don't make them choose between two brands.
- **Executor**: Licensing deals and streaming infrastructure have long lead times, so start the negotiations this week.
- **Finance** (content costs decide this): Run DVD for cash and set a fixed budget for content spend.
- **Customer** (migration risk): Keep DVD available and let usage, not price, push customers to streaming.

*Timing caveat*: In 2026 streaming is mature and crowded, and Netflix closed its own DVD service in 2023. If that's your situation, don't take on the giants head-on. Stream only a niche catalogue where you can secure the rights.
