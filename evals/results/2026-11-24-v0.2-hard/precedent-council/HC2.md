**Decision**: Whether to shut down a simple online design tool for non-designers, or keep going, given a giant competitor, dozens of investor rejections, and beta users who return weekly and invite colleagues.

**Verdict**: Don't give up. Keep going, but change the question you're answering: stop asking whether investors believe in it and start testing whether users will pay · Confidence: 7/10

**Assumptions** (you gave no numbers): the beta has dozens to hundreds of users, the tool is free today, and your runway is measured in months, not years.

**Precedents**
✓ Canva: same market (easy design for non-designers), same business model (browser-based, spread by users), same trap (Adobe as the incumbent). Melanie Perkins has said publicly that more than 100 investors turned her down. She kept going because the product was getting real use.
✓ Figma: competed with Adobe directly with a browser-based tool that spread through teams. Adobe ended up offering $20B to acquire it (the deal was abandoned in 2023). It differs from you on market: Figma serves professional designers.
☠ I found no well-documented failure that matches at least two of your dimensions, so I'm not naming one. The library's closest case is Quibi, which faced heavy competition but died because users never formed a habit. That's the opposite of what you're seeing, so it isn't a warning for you.

**Agreement**: Users coming back every week and inviting colleagues without being asked is the strongest evidence you have, and it outweighs investor rejections. Investors are judging the size of the outcome and the competition. They aren't judging whether users want the product.
**Real disagreement**: The Devil's advocate says your real competitor is no longer Adobe but Canva and Adobe Express, which are free and good enough. Retention in a free beta doesn't prove anyone will pay. The Expansionist says colleague invites point to a team or workplace wedge the giants serve poorly.
**Assumption that matters most**: That some of these users will pay rather than switch to a free alternative. → Cheapest test: ask your 20 most active users to pay for an annual plan this week, and count actual payments, not "yes" answers.
**Closest precedent**: Canva. You match its users and its rejection story. You don't match its timing: Canva entered a gap, and today that space is crowded with free tools. You need a narrower niche than "design for non-designers".

**Next step (48 h)**: Export your retention and invite data, then find which job and which type of team drive the repeat use. Send a paid offer to the top 20 users.
**Kill criterion**: If fewer than 5 of those 20 pay, and you can't find a niche where at least 3 do, by 31 January 2027, change course. Pivot the niche or wind down. Investor "no"s don't count toward this decision.
