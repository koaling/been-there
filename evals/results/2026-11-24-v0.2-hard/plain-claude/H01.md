**Short answer: not like this.** The idea may be fine. The plan rests on one number, "€8/day net, paid back in two months", and that number has sunk better-funded companies than yours. Here's how a council of five would see it.

---

### 1. The CFO: "What does 'net' actually mean?"
€8/day × 60 days = €480, so the two-month payback only works if €8 is what's left after **every** per-scooter cost. Check whether it really covers:
- **Charging and rebalancing.** This is usually the biggest cost line, whether you use staff or gig "juicers".
- **Repairs, damage, vandalism and theft.** Losses of 10–20% a year are common, more in some cities.
- **City fees.** Many cities charge per vehicle per day, per ride, or through a licence tender.
- **Insurance, payment fees, customer support, app and IoT connectivity.**
- **Warehouses and local ops staff in every city.**

If €8 is really *gross ride revenue per scooter*, true contribution may be €2–4/day or less. That turns a two-month payback into eight months or more.

### 2. The Operator: "How long does a scooter actually live?"
The whole model depends on scooter lifespan. Early sharing scooters lasted *weeks*, not years. Current shared-fleet models (swappable batteries, reinforced frames) are much better, but you need **your own** pilot data on:
- Average days in service before a scooter is written off
- Share of the fleet that is rideable on any given day (often 60–80%, not 100%)
- Repair cost per scooter per month

If a €500 scooter lasts 8 months at €3/day real contribution, it makes money, but only just. If it lasts 4 months, it loses money.

### 3. The Skeptic: "Your pilot isn't representative."
- **Novelty effect.** Demand in a launch city usually peaks early and then settles.
- **Utilisation dilution.** Rides per scooter fall as you add scooters. If the pilot had 300 scooters, demand for 3,000 is a different question.
- **Seasonality.** When did the pilot run? Summer numbers don't hold up in a northern European February.
- **Selection bias.** You probably piloted in your best city. City #37 won't look like it.
- **Competition.** Lime, Dott/Tier, Bolt and Voi are already in most attractive European cities. They have scale, data and relationships with city halls.

### 4. The Regulator: "You don't control market access."
This is the biggest risk. European cities increasingly use **tenders with fleet caps** (often 2–3 operators, with a fixed number of vehicles each). Some have restricted or banned shared scooters outright; Paris voted them out in 2023, and Madrid and Barcelona have tightened rules. In practice:
- You may **win licences in far fewer than 40 cities**, and get fewer scooters per city than you planned.
- Tenders take months and judge you on safety, parking compliance and local operations, not just price.
- Ordering 50,000 scooters *before* you have licences means you could be stuck with a warehouse full of inventory you can't deploy.

### 5. The Strategist: "Even if the unit economics work, the plan is too big."
- **50,000 × €500 = €25M** in hardware alone. Add ops, staff and launch marketing in 40 cities and you're probably at **€40–60M**. Where is that money coming from, and what happens if payback takes three times longer?
- **Operational complexity.** Launching 40 cities in a year means hiring and running roughly 40 local teams, warehouses and supply chains. Most failures in this sector came from operations, not demand.
- **Precedent.** The sector's history (Bird's bankruptcy, many consolidations and exits) is mostly a story of fast expansion on optimistic per-unit maths.

---

### Council verdict: **No-go on 50k / 40 cities. Go on a staged rollout.**

**Phase 1 (next 3–6 months)**
- Rebuild the unit economics with fully loaded costs from the pilot: real lifespan, real availability, real cost per charge and per repair.
- Run the pilot through a low season.
- Apply for licences in 3–5 carefully chosen cities.

**Phase 2: expand only if all of these hold**
- Contribution per scooter of at least €X/day after *all* direct costs (you set X, with margin to spare)
- Scooter lifespan of at least 12 months, measured, not promised by the manufacturer
- Payback of 6 months or less in a *second* city, not only the pilot
- Licences secured before any hardware order for a city

**Phase 3**
- Scale to 10–15 cities with a phased hardware order, or with leasing or vendor financing so you don't buy 50k units upfront.
- Use deposits or options with the manufacturer so you can grow the order once the numbers hold.

**Bottom line:** the demand looks promising. What isn't proven yet is that the unit economics hold up across many cities and through a whole year. Prove them in 3–5 cities, then scale with capital you can afford to lose if the numbers are wrong.
