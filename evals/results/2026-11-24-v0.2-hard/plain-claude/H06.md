**Short verdict: No. As described, this is the plan that killed Fast.** Fast was a one-click checkout startup. It raised about $100M+ on merchant signups, grew to roughly 400 people, and was reported to have about $600K in annual revenue. It shut down in 2022 when the next round didn't come. You are describing almost exactly the same company. Here's how the council sees it.

---

### The VC (the person you're pitching)
"Signed merchants" is a vanity metric in payments. What I'll ask for is **GMV processed, take rate, and live merchants, meaning merchants with real transactions in the last 30 days.** A signed contract with no integration is a letter of intent. In 2026's funding climate, nobody prices a round on signups alone. If your deck leads with merchant count and buries revenue, I'll assume the revenue numbers are bad. Then the diligence call will confirm it.

### The CFO
Do the math out loud: **400 people means roughly $40–60M a year in burn** before infrastructure and marketing. How many months of runway do you have? If revenue is "small," your burn multiple (net burn ÷ net new ARR) is probably above 10x, and investors will flag that immediately. You haven't scaled. You've staffed up for scale before you have the economics to support it.

### The Operator / Head of Product
"Merchants take a while to integrate, that's normal." Partly true. But a long integration lag is a **product problem you can measure and fix**, not a law of nature. Questions to ask:
- What % of signed merchants are live? What's the median time-to-live?
- Of the live ones, what share of their checkout volume goes through you?
- Is integration slow because of engineering effort on their side, or because they don't prioritize it? The second is worse. It means they don't believe you'll move their conversion numbers.

One-click checkout has a network-effects problem. It only works if shoppers already have accounts. Without consumer adoption, merchants see low usage even after integrating.

### The Merchant (your customer)
"I signed because it was free and low-risk. I haven't integrated because Shop Pay / Apple Pay / PayPal / Stripe Link already cover most of what I need, and my dev team has other priorities." Signing up was cheap for them. Integrating is the real commitment, and that's the signal that matters.

### The Skeptic
The narrative "we're ready for scale" has the order backwards. You scale *after* you've shown a repeatable engine: signed → live → transacting → retained. Right now you've proven that sales can get signatures. That's the cheapest step in the funnel.

### The Optimist (to be fair)
Hundreds of signed merchants does show real demand and a working sales motion. If a cohort of live merchants shows strong conversion lift and growing volume, you have a real story. It's just a different story from "look at our merchant count."

---

### What the council recommends instead
1. **Find your true metrics now**, before investors do: live merchants, GMV, net revenue, time-to-live, and conversion lift per merchant.
2. **Cut burn hard.** Size the team to the revenue curve you actually have, and aim for 24+ months of runway. It's painful, but it's the only way you get to choose when you raise.
3. **Put everything into activation.** Use forward-deployed engineers, white-glove integrations, and partnerships with platforms (Shopify, WooCommerce, commercetools) so integration takes days, not months.
4. **Build the pitch around a cohort.** For example: "Our 30 live merchants grew GMV X% month over month and saw Y% conversion lift." Treat merchant count as pipeline, not proof.
5. **If revenue can't be shown soon,** look at a bridge from existing investors or a strategic deal, and don't stake everything on a big round.

**Bottom line:** Raise on traction, not signatures. If revenue is small, the job for the next 6–9 months is to turn signed merchants into transacting merchants while cutting burn so you survive long enough to do it.
