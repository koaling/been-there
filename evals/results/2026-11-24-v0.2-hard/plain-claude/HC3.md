## The Council: Should gaming become its own site?

I asked five advisors with different priorities to look at the idea, then wrote a verdict.

---

**The Growth Strategist: "Follow the energy, but you don't need a new site to do it."**
A stagnating core plus one category growing every month is a classic pivot signal. Many successful platforms began as a general product, then committed to the one segment that worked. Twitch was spun out of Justin.tv for this reason. The real lesson is to *focus* on the segment. A second site is only one way to do that, and it isn't the obvious one. What matters is putting gaming first, and you can do that on the platform you already have.

**The Operator: "Splitting a small team is the most expensive thing on this list."**
Two sites means two codebases or a shared one with forks, two deploy pipelines, two sets of moderation queues, two support inboxes, two SEO efforts and two brands to market. A small team that splits ends up with two half-teams. Neither can ship fast enough to win. The general site will keep declining, and now with less attention. Gaming will grow more slowly than it would have with everyone behind it. You would be adding overhead exactly when you need speed.

**The Marketplace Economist: "Live streaming is a two-sided network. Don't cut your network in half."**
Streamers go where viewers are, and viewers go where streamers are. Today your gaming viewers can discover other content and your other viewers can discover gaming. A separate site resets liquidity on both sides. You'd have to migrate streamers, rebuild follower graphs, move accounts and payments, and you'd probably lose a share of people at each step. You'd also give up SEO and domain authority you've already built. Before splitting, check how much gaming growth comes from viewers who *came in through* the general site.

**The Skeptic: "Hours watched isn't the same as a business."**
Long watch sessions are great for engagement and bad for bandwidth bills. Before you commit, answer these:
- Is **revenue per viewer-hour** in gaming higher or lower than in other categories? Gaming audiences often skew toward ad-blocking and low ARPU unless tipping or subscriptions are strong.
- Is gaming growth coming from **a few big streamers**? If two of them leave, does the curve flatten?
- Why would gamers choose you over **Twitch, YouTube and Kick**? Inside a general site, gaming is a differentiated category. On its own it's a small competitor in the most crowded segment of live streaming. You need a sharp angle: a niche game genre, a region or language, better revenue share for streamers, or better community tools.

**The Brand Advocate: "There is a real case for a separate identity."**
Gamers are tribal. A general-purpose brand can feel like "not for us," and a dedicated identity could convert better and attract sponsors and esports partners. That's a legitimate reason, but it's a *branding and front-end* issue, not an infrastructure or team issue.

---

## Verdict: Focus on gaming, but don't split the company

**Don't build a separate site with a separate team. Do reposition around gaming.** Here's the order I'd go in:

1. **Make gaming the hero of the existing platform.** Put it first on the homepage, in discovery, onboarding and marketing. Point the roadmap at gaming-specific features like game directories, clips, raids and hosting, and in-stream overlays. Keep other categories running, but put them in maintenance mode.
2. **If you want brand separation, use a "skin," not a fork.** Launch a gaming-branded domain or subdomain on the **same backend, the same accounts and the same team**. Users sign in once, streamers keep their followers, and you get a gaming identity without twice the overhead.
3. **Run it as a 60–90 day experiment with kill criteria** set in advance, for example:
   - Gaming revenue per viewer-hour ≥ site average
   - Growth in new streamers on the gaming side, not just viewers
   - Retention of gaming viewers who arrive directly vs. via the general site
   - No sharp drop in overall revenue from deprioritizing other categories
4. **Only consider a full spin-off later**, and only if gaming has clearly become the business, with enough revenue to hire a dedicated team. At that point the right move is probably to *rebrand the whole company around gaming*, not to run two sites side by side.

**In short:** your data says "focus on gaming," not "build a second company." With a small team, focus beats fragmentation. Make gaming the main product on the platform you already have, and let the numbers tell you whether it deserves its own name.
