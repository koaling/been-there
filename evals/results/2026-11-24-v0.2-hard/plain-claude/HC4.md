## The Council: Should we go hard on streaming while DVD is still the profit engine?

I've put five advisors around the table. Each one argues from their own angle, and then I give you a verdict.

---

### 1. The Strategist: "Yes. Cannibalize yourself before someone else does."
This is a textbook innovator's dilemma. DVD-by-mail has a ceiling built in: postage, warehouses, physical discs, and a one-to-two-day delay that customers will stop accepting once something instant is good enough. Streaming has almost no marginal cost per view, and its quality improves every year as broadband spreads. Small but growing is exactly how disruptions look early on. If you protect DVD, a competitor with nothing to lose (a tech giant, a studio, a well-funded startup) takes streaming, and you end up as the best mail-order company in a market that's shrinking.
**Position:** Push hard. Your best customers should be the first ones you move.

### 2. The CFO: "Yes, but DVD pays for it, so don't kill it early."
DVD is the cash machine that funds the transition. The worst thing you could do is starve it before streaming can stand on its own. Streaming has a different cost structure: content licensing is a large fixed cost that keeps rising, and studios will push prices up once they see streaming matter. Model it carefully:
- Profit per subscriber in DVD vs. streaming (today and in 3 years)
- How licensing costs grow as you scale
- Shipping cost savings each time a user moves from discs to streaming

**Position:** Go aggressively on streaming, and run DVD for cash, not growth. Stop investing in DVD expansion, but keep it efficient and profitable for as long as it lasts.

### 3. The Customer Advocate: "Make the move feel like a gift, not a price increase."
Customers don't care about your business model. They care about selection, convenience and price. Right now the streaming catalog is probably thinner than the DVD catalog, so forcing people over will feel like a downgrade. Bundle streaming into existing plans at no extra cost and let usage move on its own. What you must not do is split the service and raise prices all at once. That kind of move can trigger a wave of cancellations and do lasting damage to the brand.
**Position:** Push streaming through value, not force. Communicate any changes carefully and give people time.

### 4. The Skeptic: "Who controls the content?"
In DVD you benefit from the first-sale doctrine: once you buy a disc, you can rent it out without asking the studio. Streaming has no such right. You need a license for every title, and the studios hold the leverage. If streaming becomes your main business, your supplier can squeeze your margins or pull titles to launch its own service. Today's profitable DVD business could become a low-margin streaming business that depends on companies that will end up competing with you.
**Position:** Don't bet everything on streaming until you have a plan to reduce that dependency, such as exclusive licenses, original content, or both.

### 5. The Operator: "This is an organizational problem as much as a strategic one."
If streaming sits inside the DVD organization, the DVD side will win every argument about budget, homepage space and talent, because that's where the money is today. Give streaming its own leader, its own metrics (engagement, hours watched, retention) and its own budget. Also invest in getting onto devices: TVs, consoles, set-top boxes. A streaming service that only works on a laptop stays a niche product.
**Position:** Separate the teams, give streaming real autonomy, and make device reach a top priority.

---

### Where they agree
- Streaming is the future, and DVD is a declining asset with an expiry date nobody knows yet.
- The question isn't **whether** to go into streaming. It's **how fast** and **how you fund** the move.
- Cannibalizing yourself is better than being cannibalized by someone else.

### Where they disagree
- **Speed:** The Strategist wants to go all in now. The CFO and the Skeptic want to scale in stages, tied to unit economics and content security.
- **Separating the businesses:** The Operator wants separate teams internally. The Customer Advocate warns against splitting the product in a way customers notice.

---

### Verdict
**Yes, push streaming hard, and treat DVD as the bank that funds it, not as the business you're protecting.**

Concrete plan:
1. **Bundle first:** add streaming to existing plans at no extra cost to drive adoption and learn from real usage.
2. **Harvest DVD:** no new growth investment. Optimize operations and keep the cash flow.
3. **Separate the organization internally**, with streaming-specific KPIs and a leader who doesn't report to the DVD business.
4. **Lock in content:** sign multi-year licenses now while studios still underestimate streaming, and start exploring original content for the longer term.
5. **Get on every screen:** TVs, consoles, devices. Reach matters more than features.
6. **Avoid sudden moves:** if you ever split the plans or change prices, do it in stages with plenty of notice. Your subscribers' trust is the asset that carries over from DVD to streaming.

**Biggest risk if you push:** margin compression and dependence on studios.
**Biggest risk if you don't:** becoming irrelevant within 5 to 10 years. The second one is worse.
