**Decision**: Whether to convert all ~400 freelance assistants to full-time employees next quarter at unchanged prices, or take a staged, repriced route. This is close to a one-way door: hiring 400 people is easy, but undoing it means layoffs, severance and reputational damage.
**Verdict**: Not as planned. Do it like this: price it first, convert in waves, reprice new customers · Confidence: 7/10

**Agreement**: The goal is sound. Misclassification risk is real and consistency drives quality. The problem is "all 400, one quarter, same price." The plan quietly assumes three things that you didn't mention:
1. **Cost per assistant.** Employer taxes, benefits and paid leave typically add roughly 20–40% on top of pay (an estimate, and it varies by country).
2. **Utilisation.** Today you pay freelancers for billed hours. Employees get paid for 40 hours whether or not clients use them, so every idle hour becomes your cost.
3. **Geography.** If the 400 work across several countries, you need entities or an Employer of Record (EOR) in each one. That costs money and won't happen in a quarter.

**Real disagreement**: The Expansionist argues that employees mean lower churn, better quality and a premium story, so the conversion pays for itself. That's only true if customer retention measurably improves, and you have no data on that yet.

**Assumption that matters most**: Gross margin per customer after conversion, at current prices and current utilisation. → To test it cheaply, build a 1-day spreadsheet that takes each assistant's billed hours from the last 3 months and applies the fully loaded employee cost.
