**Decision**: Decide whether to go ahead with a big national launch next month for a proximity-only photo app (no friends list) or change how you launch, given €40M raised and no live users yet.
**Verdict**: Don't do the national launch. Do it like this: launch in 2–3 dense, closed places first (campuses, festivals, venues), and make sharing opt-in per moment. · Confidence: 8/10

**Agreement**: Every seat lands on the same weak point. The app only works when other active users are within range at the same moment. A national launch spreads users thin, so most people open it, see an empty feed and never come back. Great tech and a great team don't fix an empty room.

**Real disagreement**: The Expansionist says the €40M is a weapon: you can pay to seed density (events, ambassadors) that smaller rivals can't. The Devil's advocate says the money is the trap, because it pays for a big launch before you have proof. The chair sides with "use the money to buy density in one place, not reach everywhere."

**Assumption that matters most**: That on a typical open, enough other users are nearby to fill the feed. Two quieter assumptions sit behind it. First, that people want strangers to see their photos automatically (assumed EU/Spain: photos of identifiable people are personal data under GDPR, and venues will include minors). Second, that users come back after the novelty wears off. → Cheapest test: a closed pilot on one campus or at one event, measuring the share of sessions with at least one nearby stranger's photo, plus day-7 return.

**Closest precedent**: Color (2011) — raised about $41M before launch for almost this exact product: automatic sharing of photos with people nearby. Users opened it alone, saw nothing, and left. It pivoted, then wound down within about a year and a half. You are them unless you launch dense and small.

Precedents
☠ Color (2011) — died of: no density at launch, then retention. Matches: market, model, timing, mechanism. (Not in the library; well-documented case.)

✓ Facebook (2004) — beat the same cold start by opening one campus at a time and only expanding once each was saturated.

**Next step (48 h)**: Cancel or postpone the national media buy. Pick 2–3 dense pilot sites, and set the default to "share this moment?" instead of always-on.
**Kill criterion**: If, by 8 weeks into the pilot, fewer than half of sessions show a nearby stranger's photo, or day-7 return stays under about 20% (an estimate; set your own bar before you start), stop scaling and rethink the core mechanic.
